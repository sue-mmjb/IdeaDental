What dental implants are

When you have missing teeth, an alternative to dentures is dental implants. While dentures are removable, dental implants provide permanent new teeth that restore your mouth's function and appearance, offering a lasting solution to the loss of some or all of your teeth.

An implant is a small titanium post placed in the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. Over about three to six months it fuses with the bone — a process called osseointegration — creating a stable foundation. A custom crown is then attached on top, so the finished tooth looks and works like a natural one.

Who dental implants are for

Most healthy adults with one or more missing teeth are candidates. The best candidates have:

  • Enough jawbone to support the implant (a bone graft can sometimes help if not)
  • Healthy gums
  • Good general health and oral hygiene

Factors like smoking, uncontrolled diabetes or gum disease can affect healing, so the dentist reviews your health history and takes imaging before recommending implants.

Your options

Implants can replace a single tooth or many:

  • Single tooth — one implant with a custom crown.
  • Several teeth — an implant-supported bridge.
  • Full arch — an implant-supported denture for selected patients.

What to expect

  1. Consultation & imaging. The dentist examines your mouth, evaluates the bone, and discusses your options.
  2. Implant placement. The titanium post is placed under local anesthesia.
  3. Healing. The implant fuses with the bone over roughly three to six months; a temporary tooth may be possible in the meantime.
  4. Crown placement. Once healed and stable, your custom crown is attached.

Common questions about implants

How does a dental implant work?

A dental implant is a small titanium post placed in the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. Over about three to six months it fuses with the bone, a process called osseointegration, creating a stable foundation. A custom crown is then attached on top, giving you a replacement tooth that looks and functions like a natural one.

Can implants replace several teeth or a full arch?

Yes. A single implant with a crown can replace one tooth, while implant-supported bridges or dentures can replace several teeth or a full arch. The right option depends on how many teeth are missing and the condition of your jawbone, which the dentist evaluates at your consultation.

Am I a candidate for dental implants?

Most healthy adults with one or more missing teeth are candidates. Good candidates have enough jawbone to support the implant and healthy gums. Factors like smoking, uncontrolled diabetes or gum disease can affect healing, so the dentist reviews your health history and takes imaging before recommending implants.

Is dental implant surgery painful?

The procedure itself is done with local anesthesia, so you should not feel pain during placement. Afterward, most patients have mild swelling or soreness for a few days that is usually managed with over-the-counter pain relief and settles quickly.
