What counts as a dental emergency?

A dental emergency includes severe or persistent tooth pain, facial swelling, a knocked-out or cracked tooth, a lost filling or crown, uncontrolled bleeding, or an injury to the mouth. Call the office right away. If you have trouble breathing or swallowing, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room, as those can be life-threatening.

What should I do if my tooth gets knocked out?

Pick the tooth up by the crown, not the root, and gently rinse it without scrubbing. If you can, place it back in the socket; otherwise keep it in milk or saliva and get to a dentist as fast as possible. A tooth has the best chance of being saved when treated within about an hour, so call (832) 664-8640 immediately.

What should I do if my crown or filling falls out?

Keep the crown if you still have it and avoid chewing on that side. Do not use household glue. Call Idea Dental to arrange an evaluation, since an exposed tooth can be sensitive and is more vulnerable to damage or decay until it is repaired.

Dental implants

How does a dental implant work?

A dental implant is a small titanium post placed in the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. Over about three to six months it fuses with the bone, a process called osseointegration, creating a stable foundation. A custom crown is then attached on top, giving you a replacement tooth that looks and functions like a natural one.

Can implants replace several teeth or a full arch?

Yes. A single implant with a crown can replace one tooth, while implant-supported bridges or dentures can replace several teeth or a full arch. The right option depends on how many teeth are missing and the condition of your jawbone, which the dentist evaluates at your consultation.

Am I a candidate for dental implants?

Most healthy adults with one or more missing teeth are candidates. Good candidates have enough jawbone to support the implant and healthy gums. Factors like smoking, uncontrolled diabetes or gum disease can affect healing, so the dentist reviews your health history and takes imaging before recommending implants.

Is dental implant surgery painful?

The procedure itself is done with local anesthesia, so you should not feel pain during placement. Afterward, most patients have mild swelling or soreness for a few days that is usually managed with over-the-counter pain relief and settles quickly.

Braces & Invisalign

What is Invisalign and how does it work?

Invisalign is a way to straighten teeth using a series of clear, custom-made plastic aligners instead of metal braces. You wear each set for about one to two weeks, and they gradually move your teeth into place. The aligners are removable, so you can eat, brush and floss normally, and most people won't notice you're wearing them.

Is Invisalign better than braces?

Neither is universally better; it depends on your case. Invisalign is popular with teens and adults because it is nearly invisible and removable, while traditional or clear braces can handle more complex tooth movements. At your consultation the dentist can use the iTero scanner to show what Invisalign results could look like and recommend the best fit.

What is the iTero scanner?

The iTero scanner is a digital 3D scanner Idea Dental uses to create precise images of your teeth without the messy, uncomfortable putty impressions of the past. It makes appointments more comfortable and can also preview the results you might achieve with Invisalign before you begin treatment.

What types of braces do you offer?

Idea Dental offers traditional metal braces, clear (tooth-colored) braces, and clear aligners such as Invisalign, including options for teens and adults. Traditional braces remain the most reliable choice for complex cases, while clear braces and aligners are less noticeable. The orthodontic team helps you choose based on your goals and budget.

At what age should my child see an orthodontist?

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a child first see an orthodontist by around age seven. At that age treatment often isn't needed yet, but an early check lets the dentist monitor how the teeth and jaw are developing and catch problems while they are simpler to correct.

Cosmetic dentistry

What are porcelain veneers?

Porcelain veneers are thin, custom-made shells bonded to the front of your teeth to improve their color, shape, size or length. They are a cosmetic option for teeth that are discolored, chipped or uneven, or for discoloration that whitening cannot fix, and they give a natural-looking, lasting result.

How long does teeth whitening last?

Professional teeth whitening results typically last several months to a couple of years, depending on your habits. Coffee, tea, red wine and smoking cause teeth to stain again faster. Good oral hygiene and occasional touch-ups help keep your smile bright longer.

General & restorative care

What is a root canal and does it hurt?

A root canal treats an infected or badly damaged tooth by removing the inflamed inner tissue, cleaning the space and sealing it, which relieves pain and saves the tooth. Modern root canals are done under anesthesia and, for most patients, feel similar to getting a filling rather than the painful reputation they once had.

Do I really need to replace a missing tooth?

Yes, replacing a missing tooth is usually recommended. When a tooth is gone, nearby teeth can shift, the jawbone can shrink over time, and chewing and speech can be affected. Options such as implants, bridges and dentures restore function and help protect your remaining teeth.

What are dentures and are there different kinds?

Dentures are removable replacements for missing teeth. Full dentures replace all the teeth in an arch, while partial dentures fill in gaps when some natural teeth remain. Idea Dental fits dentures for proper function and comfort and provides guidance on at-home care and follow-up cleanings.

How often should I have a dental check-up and cleaning?

For most people a check-up and professional cleaning every six months is recommended. Regular visits let the dentist catch small problems early, before they need more involved treatment. Some patients with gum disease or other conditions may be advised to come more often.

What happens at my first visit?

At your first visit the dentist reviews your health history, examines your teeth and gums, takes any necessary imaging, and discusses your goals. You'll receive a recommended treatment plan with the options available to you. It's a good time to ask about scheduling, insurance and payment options.
