What we treat same-day

If you're not sure whether it's an emergency, call and describe what's happening. The team will tell you what to do next. We commonly help with:

  • Severe or lasting toothache
  • Knocked-out tooth
  • Broken or cracked tooth
  • Lost or loose crown
  • Lost filling
  • Facial or gum swelling
  • Bleeding that won't stop
  • Broken braces wire or bracket

What to do right now

Knocked-out tooth

Hold it by the crown, not the root. Rinse gently, don't scrub. Put it back in the socket if you can, or keep it in milk. Best saved within about an hour, so call now.

Lost crown or filling

Keep the crown if you have it and avoid chewing on that side. Don't use household glue. Call us to arrange a repair before the tooth is damaged further.

Broken or chipped tooth

Save any pieces if you can and rinse your mouth with warm water. A cold compress helps swelling. Call for an evaluation as soon as possible.

Severe toothache

Rinse with warm water and floss gently to remove trapped food. Don't place aspirin on the gum. Over-the-counter pain relief can help until we see you.

What counts as a dental emergency?

A dental emergency includes severe or persistent tooth pain, facial swelling, a knocked-out or cracked tooth, a lost filling or crown, uncontrolled bleeding, or an injury to the mouth. Call the office right away. If you have trouble breathing or swallowing, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room, as those can be life-threatening.

What should I do if my tooth gets knocked out?

Pick the tooth up by the crown, not the root, and gently rinse it without scrubbing. If you can, place it back in the socket; otherwise keep it in milk or saliva and get to a dentist as fast as possible. A tooth has the best chance of being saved when treated within about an hour, so call (832) 664-8640 immediately.

What should I do if my crown or filling falls out?

Keep the crown if you still have it and avoid chewing on that side. Do not use household glue. Call Idea Dental to arrange an evaluation, since an exposed tooth can be sensitive and is more vulnerable to damage or decay until it is repaired.
